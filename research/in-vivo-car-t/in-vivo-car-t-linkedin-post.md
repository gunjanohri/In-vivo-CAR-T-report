# LinkedIn Post Draft

**Why is *in vivo* CAR-T getting so much attention right now?**

One reason is straightforward: in **2026**, Eli Lilly agreed to acquire Kelonia Therapeutics for **up to $7.0B**, including **$3.25B upfront** and the deal is directly tied to a clinical-stage *in vivo* CAR-T asset, not just a broad platform story.[Lilly/Kelonia](https://investor.lilly.com/news-releases/news-release-details/lilly-acquire-kelonia-therapeutics-advance-vivo-car-t-cell)

A few takeaways from a fresh review of the publicly visible *in vivo* CAR-T landscape:

1. **This is not just a new efficacy story.**
   The bigger strategic pitch is a new operating model for cell therapy: engineer immune cells inside the patient rather than collecting, manufacturing, and reinfusing them.

2. **Delivery is the chokepoint.**
   The real question is whether targeted *in vivo* gene delivery can be precise, potent, and safe enough to make the model work consistently at scale.

3. **Autoimmune disease is a major near-term wedge.**
   CD19-directed B-cell reset is conceptually attractive, and multiple developers are leaning into autoimmune programs rather than oncology alone.

4. **Oncology still matters.**
   Multiple myeloma and B-cell malignancies remain important proof-of-potency settings for the field.

5. **Big pharma is buying early.**
   AbbVie / Capstan, AstraZeneca / EsoBiotec, Kite / Interius, BMS / Orbital, Lilly / Orna, and Lilly / Kelonia all suggest that large companies do not want to wait for full clinical de-risking before taking platform positions.

6. **The field is still early.**
   Many datasets are small, company-reported, preclinical, or early Phase 1. Deal volume should not be mistaken for broad clinical validation.

The key question for the next 12-24 months:

**Can *in vivo* CAR-T retain enough of conventional CAR-T's potency and durability while meaningfully improving speed, access, and manufacturing scalability?**

If yes, the category could become more than a cell-therapy niche.
It could become a new product architecture for immune-cell engineering.

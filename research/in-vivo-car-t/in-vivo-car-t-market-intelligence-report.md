## 1. Executive summary

### Strategic read-through

- **For pharma BD:** *In vivo* CAR-T offers a manufacturing-light cell-therapy platform thesis, but diligence should center on delivery specificity, safety control, and whether early human data support useful durability.
- **For platform investors:** value is likely to concentrate around delivery IP, immune-cell targeting, CMC execution, and evidence that in-body programming can create a meaningful therapeutic effect rather than a transient biologic signal.
- **For cell therapy companies:** *In vivo* CAR-T is a strategic adjacency and hedge today, not an immediate replacement for leading *ex vivo* products.
- **For delivery technology companies:** the route to strategic importance is not the carrier alone, but proof that the carrier can program the right immune cells with a clinically usable safety and dosing profile.
- **For *ex vivo* and allogeneic incumbents:** the near-term risk is strategic rather than commercial displacement; the more immediate implication is pressure to broaden platform scope into autoimmune disease, faster-access settings, or manufacturing-light extensions.

## 2. How CAR-T works

CAR-T therapy is an engineered T-cell therapy. A CAR, or chimeric antigen receptor, is a synthetic receptor added to the T-cell surface so the cell can recognize a chosen antigen such as CD19, CD20, or BCMA. Once the CAR binds its target, the T cell activates, kills the target cell, releases immune signals, and may expand and persist.[Li et al., PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41928252/) [Bot et al., Nature Reviews Drug Discovery](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41573-025-01291-5)

### In plain English

1. A disease antigen is selected.
2. A CAR is introduced so the T cell can recognize that antigen.
3. The CAR-T cell binds an antigen-positive target cell.
4. The CAR-T cell activates and kills the target cell.
5. The CAR-T population may expand and persist for some period after dosing.

All CAR-T formats share the same core biology. What changes across autologous *ex vivo*, allogeneic *ex vivo*, and *in vivo* CAR-T is cell source, operating model, and where the engineering occurs.

## 3. *Ex Vivo* CAR-T today

*Ex vivo* CAR-T remains the clinical benchmark for engineered T-cell potency, but it is still defined by cell collection, external engineering, manufacturing, and reinfusion.

### Autologous *ex vivo* CAR-T

Autologous *ex vivo* CAR-T begins with the patient's own T cells. Cells are collected by leukapheresis, engineered outside the body, expanded and tested in a manufacturing facility, and then reinfused after lymphodepleting chemotherapy. This format underlies the currently approved autologous CAR-T class in the United States.[FDA CAR-T class overview](https://www.fda.gov/safety/medical-product-safety-information/bcma-directed-or-cd19-directed-autologous-chimeric-antigen-receptor-car-t-cell-immunotherapies-fda)

Approved examples include **Kymriah** (Novartis), **Yescarta** (Gilead/Kite), **Breyanzi** (Bristol Myers Squibb), and **Carvykti** (J&J/Legend).[FDA CAR-T class overview](https://www.fda.gov/safety/medical-product-safety-information/bcma-directed-or-cd19-directed-autologous-chimeric-antigen-receptor-car-t-cell-immunotherapies-fda) The format proved the biology, but its operating model still carries individualized manufacturing, scheduling, and access constraints.[Bot et al., Nature Reviews Drug Discovery](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41573-025-01291-5)

### Allogeneic / off-the-shelf CAR-T

Allogeneic CAR-T aims to convert CAR-T from a patient-specific product into an off-the-shelf cell product. Developers use donor-derived or stem-cell-derived cells, engineer them outside the body, manufacture them in batches, and hold inventory for future use. The tradeoff is that the model inherits donor-cell liabilities such as rejection, graft-versus-host disease risk, and a heavier dependence on gene editing and immune-evasion engineering.[Allogene overview](https://allogene.com/about-us) [Caribou pipeline](https://www.cariboubio.com/pipeline/) [Cellectis platform](https://www.cellectis.com/)

- **Allogene Therapeutics:** clinical-stage allogeneic CAR-T developer with **cema-cel** and autoimmune-focused **ALLO-329**.[Allogene website](https://allogene.com/) [Allogene 2026 update](https://ir.allogene.com/news-releases/news-release-details/allogene-therapeutics-positions-2026-program-defining-year)
- **Caribou Biosciences:** clinical-stage allogeneic CAR-T developer with **vispa-cel (CB-010)** and **CB-011**.[Caribou pipeline](https://www.cariboubio.com/pipeline/)
- **Cellectis:** gene-editing platform company focused on allogeneic CAR-T and off-the-shelf manufacturing.[Cellectis platform](https://www.cellectis.com/)

### CAR-T modality comparison

| Modality | How it works | Core advantage | Main bottleneck | Manufacturing/access implication | Strategic relevance |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Autologous *ex vivo* CAR-T | Patient T cells are collected, engineered outside the body, expanded, and reinfused | Clinically validated potency and persistence | Individualized manufacturing and treatment delay | Highest operational burden; depends on apheresis, manufacturing slots, and cold chain | Current commercial benchmark |
| Allogeneic *ex vivo* CAR-T | Donor- or stem-cell-derived immune cells are engineered in batches and held as inventory | Off-the-shelf availability and more standardized production | Rejection, graft-versus-host disease risk, persistence, and heavy editing needs | Faster access if inventory exists, but still a cell-manufacturing model | Strategic hedge on off-the-shelf cell therapy |
| *In vivo* CAR-T | CAR instructions are delivered directly into endogenous immune cells inside the patient | Removes external cell manufacturing and could simplify access | Delivery specificity, dose control, safety, and durability | Most manufacturing-light model if it works; complexity shifts to carrier and CMC | High-option platform bet, not yet a clinically proven replacement |

## 4. *In Vivo* CAR-T as a delivery model

*In vivo* CAR-T aims to generate CAR-T cells directly inside the patient. Instead of collecting cells and engineering them externally, a delivery system such as a targeted lipid nanoparticle or retargeted viral vector carries CAR-encoding instructions into endogenous immune cells. If the right cells are reached and engineered safely, the result is an injectable genetic medicine that creates CAR-T cells in situ.[Bot et al., Nature Reviews Drug Discovery](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41573-025-01291-5) [Li et al., PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41928252/)

The strategic appeal is not a new version of CAR biology; it is a different operating model. Relative to autologous and allogeneic *ex vivo* CAR-T, the category removes external cell manufacturing and donor-cell biology, but shifts the main risk to delivery specificity, dose control, safety, and durability.

## 5. Where *in vivo* CAR-T could ease *ex vivo* constraints

The strategic promise of *in vivo* CAR-T is that it could solve part of the cell-therapy operating-model problem, not just the target-recognition problem. None of the advantages below should be read as proven class benefits yet.

| Constraint | Why it matters | Where *in vivo* may help | What still has to be proven |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Complex external manufacturing | Cell collection, engineering, expansion, testing, and release create a slow and operationally fragile process | Replaces bespoke cell production with a stockable delivery product | Whether the carrier can be manufactured and dosed reproducibly at scale |
| Long treatment lead time | Delays matter in fast-progressing disease and constrain site throughput | Could shorten treatment to a standard dosing schedule if supply is ready | Whether real-world monitoring and site requirements still limit speed |
| High cost and cold-chain logistics | Personalized lots and specialized handling limit access and gross margin | A vial-based product should be easier to ship and schedule than a cell lot | Whether efficacy justifies a meaningfully simpler cost structure |
| Variable cell source quality | Heavily pretreated patients may have weak starting cells, while off-the-shelf formats add donor-cell quality considerations | Avoids external expansion and may program cells in their native environment | Patient biology still varies, and poor endogenous T-cell fitness may still matter |
| Conditioning burden | Lymphodepletion adds toxicity, coordination, and inpatient complexity | Several *in vivo* programs are explicitly designed to avoid chemotherapy preconditioning | Avoiding conditioning is still a program-level design goal, not a class effect.[Capstan Phase 1](https://www.capstantx.com/press-releases/capstan-therapeutics-announces-initiation-of-phase-1-trial-of-lead-in-vivo-car-t-therapy-cptx2309-for-treating-autoimmune-disease/) [Kelonia ASH 2025](https://keloniatx.com/kelonia-therapeutics-presents-first-in-human-data-from-phase-1-inmmycar-study-of-kln-1010-in-vivo-bcma-car-t-therapy-at-the-american-society-of-hematology-ash-2025-annual-meeting/) [Interius first patient dosed](https://interiusbio.com/press-release/interius-biotherapeutics-doses-first-patient-with-in-vivo-chimeric-antigen-receptor-car-gene-therapy-for-b-cell-malignancies/) |
| Off-the-shelf donor-cell constraints | Allogeneic formats still face rejection, graft-versus-host disease risk, persistence questions, and editing complexity | *In vivo* uses endogenous cells and may avoid multi-edit donor-cell engineering | Delivery complexity may simply replace donor-cell complexity rather than remove it |

## 7. Delivery methods and strategic implications for *in vivo* CAR-T

Delivery is the strategic chokepoint for *in vivo* CAR-T because the carrier determines whether CAR instructions reach the right immune cells at a clinically meaningful dose, with acceptable safety, controllability, and durability. In practice, delivery IP may matter as much as the CAR construct itself.[Bot et al., Nature Reviews Drug Discovery](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41573-025-01291-5) [Li et al., PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41928252/)

| Delivery approach | Intended advantage | Key risk or limitation | Strategic implication | Representative companies |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Targeted LNP + linear mRNA | Non-integrating, titratable, and potentially compatible with repeat dosing | Transient expression and off-target uptake risk | Strong fit for controllable autoimmune cell reset if activity is durable enough | Capstan |
| Circular RNA + targeted LNP | Longer expression than standard mRNA without genomic integration | Targeting, redosing, and CMC remain unresolved | Could become a middle ground between transient RNA and integrating vectors | Orna, Orbital |
| Retargeted lentiviral vectors | Potentially durable one-time programming of endogenous immune cells | Insertional safety, vector immunity, and redosing difficulty | Strongest pitch for durable oncology potency, but highest long-term follow-up burden | Kelonia, Interius, Umoja, Vyriad, EsoBiotec |
| AAV-assisted targeted integration / dual-vector systems | Supports site-specific insertion and potentially more controlled editing outcomes | Multi-component complexity and manufacturing burden | Precision may be differentiated, but CMC and regulatory burden are likely high | Azalea |
| Polymeric / other non-viral nanoparticles | Broadens the non-viral toolkit beyond LNPs | Public clinical validation remains limited | Better viewed today as enabling technology than as a de-risked platform class | Mainly preclinical academic work |
| Antibody-guided or ligand-targeted delivery layers | Improves cell specificity and therapeutic index across carriers | Adds binder design and manufacturability complexity | Targeting architecture may become a major source of platform differentiation regardless of payload | Capstan, Kelonia, Vyriad |

## 8. Challenges and risks for *in vivo* CAR-T

Even if the operating-model thesis proves attractive, the technical bar remains high.

- **Cell-specific targeting and efficiency:** the platform must reach the intended immune cells with enough efficiency to generate clinically useful CAR-T activity, while avoiding clinically relevant uptake in the wrong cells.[Bot et al., Nature Reviews Drug Discovery](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41573-025-01291-5)
- **Expression control and durability:** durable expression may help efficacy but reduce controllability; transient expression may improve control but weaken persistence relative to best-in-class *ex vivo* products.
- **Immunogenicity and repeat dosing:** RNA-based systems may allow redosing in principle, but anti-carrier or anti-payload responses may narrow that advantage in practice.
- **Safety and reversibility:** once cells are programmed in the body, not all platforms have clean reversal or safety-switch options, and immune toxicities remain relevant because the downstream biology is still CAR-T biology.[FDA CAR-T class overview](https://www.fda.gov/safety/medical-product-safety-information/bcma-directed-or-cd19-directed-autologous-chimeric-antigen-receptor-car-t-cell-immunotherapies-fda)
- **CMC and regulatory burden:** even if cell manufacturing is removed, complexity shifts to the vector or nanoparticle platform, including biodistribution, potency assays, long-term follow-up, and consistency of the delivery vehicle.[Li et al., PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41928252/)
- **Clinical proof-of-concept gap:** most public programs are still at Phase 1, clinical-trial-ready, or preclinical / IND-enabling stages, so commercial readiness should not be inferred from deal volume alone.[ClinicalTrials.gov NCT07075185](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07075185) [ClinicalTrials.gov NCT06917742](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06917742) [ClinicalTrials.gov NCT06539338](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06539338)

### What the deal activity suggests

- 2024 dealmaking centered on platform access, options, and enabling components; 2025-2026 shifted toward ownership of platform builders.
- Larger buyers appear willing to pay early for delivery optionality, especially in targeted RNA / LNP and lentiviral systems, before broad clinical proof is available.
- Autoimmune programs expanded the strategic narrative beyond oncology, but oncology still matters where durable expression or first-in-human potency data are emerging.
- Deal volume validates strategic interest; it does not yet prove broad clinical or commercial success.

## 11. Conclusion

- *In vivo* CAR-T is not yet a proven commercial replacement for *ex vivo* CAR-T, but it is emerging as a strategic platform bet because it targets the biggest limitations of current cell therapy: manufacturing complexity, access, scalability, and cost.
- The next value inflection will depend less on the CAR construct alone than on targeted delivery, transduction efficiency, safety control, and durable clinical effect.
- The most strategically relevant public platforms today are the ones that combine a credible delivery thesis with a clear use-case strategy in autoimmune disease, oncology, or both; the most visible examples are Kelonia, Capstan, Interius, Umoja, Orna, Orbital, and EsoBiotec.
- The near-term question for strategy, BD, CI, and investor-facing readers is not whether the category is already de-risked. It is whether a given platform can generate selective, manufacturable, and clinically durable in-body engineering before the most useful delivery architectures are consolidated through partnering or M&A.

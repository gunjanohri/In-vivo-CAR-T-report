## 2. Strategic recommendation

Treat *in vivo* CAR-T as a high-option platform bet, not a near-term replacement for approved *ex vivo* CAR-T products.

BD and strategy teams should prioritize platforms with evidence of selective immune-cell targeting, manageable safety, reduced conditioning burden, scalable CMC and either durable clinical effect or credible repeat-dosing logic.[Bot et al., Nature Reviews Drug Discovery](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41573-025-01291-5) [ClinicalTrials.gov NCT06917742](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06917742) [ClinicalTrials.gov NCT07075185](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07075185) [ClinicalTrials.gov NCT06539338](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06539338)

Near-term interest is likely to concentrate around two use cases: autoimmune B-cell reset, where manufacturing-light delivery could expand access, plus hematologic oncology proof-of-potency programs, where early human data can validate whether in-body CAR generation is clinically meaningful.[Capstan Phase 1](https://www.capstantx.com/press-releases/capstan-therapeutics-announces-initiation-of-phase-1-trial-of-lead-in-vivo-car-t-therapy-cptx2309-for-treating-autoimmune-disease/) [Kelonia ASH 2025](https://keloniatx.com/kelonia-therapeutics-presents-first-in-human-data-from-phase-1-inmmycar-study-of-kln-1010-in-vivo-bcma-car-t-therapy-at-the-american-society-of-hematology-ash-2025-annual-meeting/) [Interius first patient dosed](https://interiusbio.com/press-release/interius-biotherapeutics-doses-first-patient-with-in-vivo-chimeric-antigen-receptor-car-gene-therapy-for-b-cell-malignancies/)

### Strategic read-through

- **For pharma BD:** *In vivo* CAR-T is a platform ownership opportunity, but diligence should center on delivery specificity, safety control and whether early human data support useful durability.
- **For platform investors:** value is likely to concentrate around delivery IP, immune-cell targeting and evidence that in-body programming can create a meaningful therapeutic effect rather than a transient biologic signal.
- **For cell therapy companies:** *In vivo* CAR-T is a strategic adjacency and hedge today, not an immediate replacement for leading *ex vivo* products.
- **For delivery technology companies:** platform value depends on proof that the carrier can program the right immune cells with a clinically usable safety and dosing profile.
- **For *ex vivo* incumbents:** the near-term risk is strategic rather than commercial displacement, with the sharper pressure on autoimmune expansion, faster-access settings and manufacturing-light extensions.

## 3. Why *in vivo* CAR-T matters now

*In vivo* CAR-T is back in focus because it targets the same clinical category that current CAR-T validated, but attacks the part of the model that remains hardest to scale: external cell manufacturing.[FDA CAR-T class overview](https://www.fda.gov/safety/medical-product-safety-information/bcma-directed-or-cd19-directed-autologous-chimeric-antigen-receptor-car-t-cell-immunotherapies-fda) [Bot et al., Nature Reviews Drug Discovery](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41573-025-01291-5)

Autologous *ex vivo* CAR-T proved the biology of engineered T-cell therapy, but it still depends on individualized manufacturing, cold-chain logistics and specialized treatment-center coordination.[FDA CAR-T class overview](https://www.fda.gov/safety/medical-product-safety-information/bcma-directed-or-cd19-directed-autologous-chimeric-antigen-receptor-car-t-cell-immunotherapies-fda) [Bot et al., Nature Reviews Drug Discovery](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41573-025-01291-5)

Allogeneic CAR-T aims to improve access through off-the-shelf inventory, but it still remains an *ex vivo* cell-manufacturing model and inherits donor-cell liabilities such as rejection, graft-versus-host disease risk and added gene-editing complexity.[Allogene overview](https://allogene.com/about-us) [Caribou pipeline](https://www.cariboubio.com/pipeline/) [Cellectis platform](https://www.cellectis.com/)

The renewed enthusiasm is therefore strategic, not fully clinical. Recent M&A, partnerships and financings suggest that buyers and investors are willing to pay early for platform ownership where delivery technology could reshape the economics and reach of cell therapy.[Lilly/Kelonia](https://investor.lilly.com/news-releases/news-release-details/lilly-acquire-kelonia-therapeutics-advance-vivo-car-t-cell) [AbbVie/Capstan](https://news.abbvie.com/2025-06-30-AbbVie-to-Acquire-Capstan-Therapeutics%2C-Further-Strengthening-Commitment-to-Transforming-Patient-Care-in-Immunology) [BMS/Orbital](https://news.bms.com/news/corporate-financial/2025/Bristol-Myers-Squibb-Strengthens-and-Diversifies-Cell-Therapy-Portfolio-with-Acquisition-of-Orbital-Therapeutics/default.aspx) [Kite/Interius](https://investors.gilead.com/news/news-details/2025/Kite-to-Acquire-Interius-BioTherapeutics-to-Advance-In-Vivo-Platform/default.aspx) [EsoBiotec/AstraZeneca](https://www.esobiotec.com/esobiotec-to-be-acquired-by-astrazeneca-to-advance-cell-therapy-ambition/)

## 4. CAR-T modality comparison

### How CAR-T works

CAR-T therapy is an engineered T-cell therapy. A CAR, or chimeric antigen receptor, is a synthetic receptor added to the T-cell surface so the cell can recognize a chosen antigen such as CD19, CD20 or BCMA. Once the CAR binds its target, the T cell activates, kills the target cell, releases immune signals and may expand and persist.[Li et al., PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41928252/) [Bot et al., Nature Reviews Drug Discovery](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41573-025-01291-5)

1. A disease antigen is selected.
2. A CAR is introduced so the T cell can recognize that antigen.
3. The CAR-T cell binds an antigen-positive target cell.
4. The CAR-T cell activates and kills the target cell.
5. The CAR-T population may expand and persist for some period after dosing.

### *Ex vivo* CAR-T today

Autologous *ex vivo* CAR-T begins with the patient's own T cells. Cells are collected by leukapheresis, engineered outside the body, expanded and tested in a manufacturing facility, then reinfused after lymphodepleting chemotherapy.[FDA CAR-T class overview](https://www.fda.gov/safety/medical-product-safety-information/bcma-directed-or-cd19-directed-autologous-chimeric-antigen-receptor-car-t-cell-immunotherapies-fda)

Approved examples include **Kymriah** (Novartis), **Yescarta** (Gilead/Kite), **Breyanzi** (Bristol Myers Squibb) and **Carvykti** (J&J/Legend). The format proved the biology, but the operating model still carries individualized manufacturing, scheduling and access constraints.[FDA CAR-T class overview](https://www.fda.gov/safety/medical-product-safety-information/bcma-directed-or-cd19-directed-autologous-chimeric-antigen-receptor-car-t-cell-immunotherapies-fda) [Bot et al., Nature Reviews Drug Discovery](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41573-025-01291-5)

### Allogeneic / off-the-shelf CAR-T

Allogeneic CAR-T aims to convert CAR-T from a patient-specific product into an off-the-shelf cell product. Developers use donor-derived or stem-cell-derived cells, engineer them outside the body, manufacture them in batches and hold inventory for future use. Public examples include **Allogene Therapeutics** with **cema-cel** and **ALLO-329**, **Caribou Biosciences** with **vispa-cel (CB-010)** and **CB-011** and **Cellectis** as a gene-editing platform focused on allogeneic CAR-T.[Allogene overview](https://allogene.com/about-us) [Allogene 2026 update](https://ir.allogene.com/news-releases/news-release-details/allogene-therapeutics-positions-2026-program-defining-year) [Caribou pipeline](https://www.cariboubio.com/pipeline/) [Cellectis platform](https://www.cellectis.com/)

### CAR-T modality comparison

| Modality | How it works | Core advantage | Main bottleneck | Manufacturing/access implication | Strategic relevance |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Autologous *ex vivo* CAR-T | Patient T cells are collected, engineered outside the body, expanded and reinfused | Clinically validated potency and persistence | Individualized manufacturing and treatment delay | Highest operational burden; depends on apheresis, manufacturing slots and cold chain | Current commercial benchmark |
| Allogeneic *ex vivo* CAR-T | Donor- or stem-cell-derived immune cells are engineered in batches and held as inventory | Off-the-shelf availability and more standardized production | Rejection, graft-versus-host disease risk, persistence and heavy editing needs | Faster access if inventory exists, but still a cell-manufacturing model | Strategic hedge on off-the-shelf cell therapy |
| *In vivo* CAR-T | CAR instructions are delivered directly into endogenous immune cells inside the patient | Removes external cell manufacturing and could simplify access | Delivery specificity, dose control, safety and durability | Most manufacturing-light model if it works; complexity shifts to carrier and CMC | High-option platform bet, not yet a clinically proven replacement |

## 5. Delivery platform landscape

### *In vivo* CAR-T as a delivery model

*In vivo* CAR-T aims to generate CAR-T cells directly inside the patient. Instead of collecting cells and engineering them externally, a delivery system such as a targeted lipid nanoparticle or retargeted viral vector carries CAR-encoding instructions into endogenous immune cells.[Bot et al., Nature Reviews Drug Discovery](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41573-025-01291-5) [Li et al., PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41928252/)

The strategic appeal is a different operating model. Relative to autologous and allogeneic *ex vivo* CAR-T, the category removes external cell manufacturing and donor-cell biology, but shifts the main risk to delivery specificity, dose control, safety and durability.

### Delivery methods and strategic implications

Delivery is the strategic chokepoint for *in vivo* CAR-T because the carrier determines whether CAR instructions reach the right immune cells at a clinically meaningful dose, with acceptable safety, controllability and durability. In practice, delivery IP may matter as much as the CAR construct itself.[Bot et al., Nature Reviews Drug Discovery](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41573-025-01291-5) [Li et al., PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41928252/)

| Delivery approach | Intended advantage | Key risk or limitation | Strategic implication | Representative companies |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Targeted LNP + linear mRNA | Non-integrating, titratable and potentially compatible with repeat dosing | Transient expression and off-target uptake risk | Strong fit for controllable autoimmune cell reset if activity is durable enough | Capstan |
| Circular RNA + targeted LNP | Longer expression than standard mRNA without genomic integration | Targeting, redosing and CMC remain unresolved | Could become a middle ground between transient RNA and integrating vectors | Orna, Orbital |
| Retargeted lentiviral vectors | Potentially durable one-time programming of endogenous immune cells | Insertional safety, vector immunity and redosing difficulty | Strongest pitch for durable oncology potency, but highest long-term follow-up burden | Kelonia, Interius, Umoja, Vyriad, EsoBiotec |
| AAV-assisted targeted integration / dual-vector systems | Supports site-specific insertion and potentially more controlled editing outcomes | Multi-component complexity and manufacturing burden | Precision may be differentiated, but CMC and regulatory burden are likely high | Azalea |
| Polymeric / other non-viral nanoparticles | Broadens the non-viral toolkit beyond LNPs | Public clinical validation remains limited | Better viewed today as enabling technology than as a de-risked platform class | Mainly preclinical academic work |
| Antibody-guided or ligand-targeted delivery layers | Improves cell specificity and therapeutic index across carriers | Adds binder design and manufacturability complexity | Targeting architecture may become a major source of platform differentiation regardless of payload | Capstan, Kelonia, Vyriad |

## 8. Key risks and diligence questions

The operating-model thesis is attractive, but the technical bar remains high.

- **Cell-specific targeting and efficiency:** the platform must reach the intended immune cells with enough efficiency to generate clinically useful CAR-T activity, while avoiding clinically relevant uptake in the wrong cells.[Bot et al., Nature Reviews Drug Discovery](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41573-025-01291-5)
- **Expression control and durability:** durable expression may help efficacy but reduce controllability, while transient expression may improve control but weaken persistence relative to best-in-class *ex vivo* products.
- **Immunogenicity and repeat dosing:** RNA-based systems may allow redosing in principle, but anti-carrier or anti-payload responses may narrow that advantage in practice.
- **Safety and reversibility:** once cells are programmed in the body, not all platforms have clean reversal or safety-switch options and immune toxicities remain relevant because the downstream biology is still CAR-T biology.[FDA CAR-T class overview](https://www.fda.gov/safety/medical-product-safety-information/bcma-directed-or-cd19-directed-autologous-chimeric-antigen-receptor-car-t-cell-immunotherapies-fda)
- **CMC and regulatory burden:** even if cell manufacturing is removed, complexity shifts to the vector or nanoparticle platform, including biodistribution, potency assays, long-term follow-up and consistency of the delivery vehicle.[Li et al., PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41928252/)
- **Clinical proof-of-concept gap:** most public programs are still at Phase 1, clinical-trial-ready or preclinical / IND-enabling stages, so commercial readiness should not be inferred from deal volume alone.[ClinicalTrials.gov NCT07075185](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07075185) [ClinicalTrials.gov NCT06917742](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06917742) [ClinicalTrials.gov NCT06539338](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06539338)

### Key diligence questions

- Can the delivery system target the intended immune-cell population with enough specificity?
- Is off-target activity low enough to support repeatable clinical use?
- Can the platform reduce or avoid lymphodepletion?
- Does early evidence suggest durable therapeutic effect?
- Can the carrier be manufactured consistently at scale?
- Is the platform differentiated enough to justify partnership or acquisition interest?

## 9. Watchlist and next inflection points

The next 12 to 24 months should clarify which platforms deserve strategic priority. The main watchpoints are early human evidence, remaining independent platform builders and whether financing and deal activity continues to cluster around delivery differentiation rather than CAR biology alone.

### What early financings suggest

- Seed and Series A activity is funding platform-option creation around delivery and cell engineering rather than near-term commercial assets.
- Investor appetite appears strongest where a platform could reduce manufacturing burden, expand cell therapy beyond oncology or create differentiated delivery IP.
- Early financing validates interest in the theme, but not clinical differentiation.

### What to watch next

- Selective immune-cell targeting in humans
- Manageable safety with low enough off-target activity to support repeatable use
- Reduced conditioning burden, especially in autoimmune programs
- Durable clinical effect or a credible repeat-dosing logic
- Partnership or acquisition interest around remaining independent platforms such as Umoja, Vyriad and Azalea

### Final takeaway

*In vivo* CAR-T is not yet a proven commercial replacement for *ex vivo* CAR-T, but it is emerging as a strategic platform bet because it targets the biggest operating-model constraints of current cell therapy: manufacturing complexity, access, scalability and cost. The next value inflection depends less on the CAR construct alone and more on targeted delivery, transduction efficiency, safety control and durable clinical effect.
